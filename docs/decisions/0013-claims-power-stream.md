# 0013 Claims, the test, power, and the stream

One claim model serves every hypothesis. One e-value test checks every confirmatory claim, the power gate simulates that same test, and the box-wide e-LOND stream gives each confirmatory study its level.

Decision:

- **Paired values.** A row reports one value per outcome `measure` in `values.json`. The box pairs it with the reference row of the same unit and repeat: `difference` = v − v_ref; `percent` = 100 × (v / v_ref − 1); `log-ratio` = ln(v / v_ref). A pair that is not finite is dropped: `percent` needs v_ref > 0, and `log-ratio` needs both values > 0. Repeats are averaged. A log ratio suits a ratio outcome such as perplexity: +4% is ln 1.04, and a doubling and a halving are the same distance from 0.
- **Contrast.** Each hypothesis names an outcome and a contrast: condition ids and weights. A unit's value is x = Σ w_k × paired(k). `selected` stands for the selection stage's pick (0009). A unit that lacks any term is incomplete.
- **Claims.** `below m` is the bound x < m; `above m` is x > m; `within [a, b]` is the two bounds x > a and x < b (two one-sided tests; Lakens 2017). A hypothesis with no claim is descriptive: it gives estimates and no verdict. A claim holds only when every bound holds in every listed stratum (intersection-union; Berger 1982). Each part is tested at the full level, so a claim needs no alpha correction inside it. Its power falls as parts are added.
- **Observation.** The test counts clusters, not units: each cluster in a stratum gives one observation, from its complete units only. For `mean = [lo, hi]` it is the mean of the cluster's units, each clipped to [lo, hi] first, so the claim is about the clipped mean. For `quantile = q` it is the share of the cluster's units on the claimed side (x ≤ m for below, x ≥ m for above).
- **The e-value test.** Per bound and stratum, a fixed-size e-process with the `Bounded` score (0009; Waudby-Smith and Ramdas) runs over the observations in a seeded order, from the seed `test/<hypothesis>/<stratum>`. A mean bets that the mean lies on the claimed side of the margin. A quantile bets that the share exceeds q (below) or 1 − q (above). Each unit of a mean is clipped to [lo, hi] first, so a mean observation lies in the range; an observation that is not a number, such as the mean of a cluster with a NaN unit, makes that bound's e-value 0. The hypothesis e-value is the minimum over its bounds and strata, which is an e-value under the union of their nulls. An incomplete unit in scope, such as a void row, makes it 0. Hypothesis h passes when its e-value is at least 1 / (level × `alpha_share`_h). The shares sum to 1, and the study e-value that closes the stream slot is max_h `alpha_share`_h × e_h (the tree split of 0009). It reaches 1 / level exactly when some claim passes, so the stream counts a discovery only for a study with a passing claim. It is at most Σ `alpha_share`_h × e_h, so it is an e-value when every claim of the study is null.
- **Exploratory claims** are judged by the point estimate of the same statistic, with no test. Such a verdict only routes. A follow-on runs on `done`, or on `<id>.pass` or `<id>.fail` of a hypothesis with a claim. At the end of a run the box writes one `verdict` record per hypothesis: `pass`, `fail` or `none`.
- **Power gate.** `power_at` is the true value of the statistic at which the test must have its power. The user sets it at registration; it is not read from a pilot, because a pilot's effect estimate biases power (Albers and Lakens 2018). It must lie strictly on the claimed side of every bound, and inside a mean's range. The gate simulates the e-value test above. Per stratum, unit x = μ + sd × (√icc × Z_cluster + √(1 − icc) × N_unit), with standard normal draws, the stratum's declared cluster sizes, and the sd and icc of a finished study (0014). μ is `power_at` for a mean, or `power_at` − sd × z(q) for a quantile, which puts the q-quantile at `power_at`. For a mean, one draw per cluster stands for its units: a normal with sd × √(icc + (1 − icc) / m), clipped to the range. The test clips each unit first; the two agree while the units stay inside the range. 4,000 replicates run from the seed `power/<hypothesis>`, so every check gives the same answer. Power is the share of replicates in which every bound in every stratum reaches 1 / (level × share). The gate fails below `design.power`. For a mean claim, the one-sided z count is a fast floor: with fewer clusters, the gate fails without simulating. A failing gate states the simulated power, the clusters per stratum, and the smallest common count per stratum that would pass, searched up to 1,024 with the declared sizes repeated in order. A simulation that would draw more than 100 million values fails closed.
- **Stream settings.** Each log has its own e-LOND stream (Xu and Ramdas): `stream/planlog.jsonl`, and `stream/testlog.jsonl` for test studies. alpha = 0.05 and gamma = `Bounded { M = 10 }`, so the t-th slot of a stream or of a test-stream epoch (t ≤ 10) gets level 0.005 × (D + 1), where D counts the discoveries of that stream or epoch closed before the slot opened. A signed `stream.config` record fixes alpha and gamma before the first slot. `stream.open` and `stream.close` records hold the slot, the study, the level, the e-value and the discovery; the state is rebuilt by replay. `check` shows the slot and level the stream would grant now. `register` opens the slot under the log's lock, and refuses a level lower than the one checked, or level 0: a study past slot 10 of the plan stream is refused. `run` closes the slot with the study e-value. A plan has no `design.alpha`: its level depends on the box's history, so no author can type it.
- **Test stream epochs.** Test studies never count, so their stream never runs out: after each 10 slots, a signed `stream.epoch` record starts a fresh stream, and slot numbers go on counting. The plan stream never resets.

Why:

- One score, one observation rule and one combination rule cover margins, equivalence, per-stratum claims, derived contrasts and a quantile rule.
- The test counts clusters because units in one cluster are not independent.
- A z formula promises power that the e-value test does not have (0009). Simulating the box's own test sizes the study for the test it will meet.
- The shares that split a study's level over its claims, and the selection stage's rule, are fixed before any data come in, as the FDA guidance on adaptive designs asks of selection and multiplicity (FDA 2019).
- A wide mean range shrinks each bet, so it costs clusters. The range is a registration value, set from data before the study registers.

Cost: power is computed under a normal model, so a skewed outcome can differ. The sd and icc come from a smaller earlier study, which estimates the icc with few clusters.
